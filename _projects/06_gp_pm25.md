Air-quality sensors are sparse — you have readings at a scatter of locations and you want a pollution estimate everywhere, with an honest sense of how sure you are at each point. That is a Gaussian-process problem, and this project builds one to predict **PM2.5** concentrations across a city, with calibrated uncertainty rather than a bare point estimate.

What makes it more than a curve-fitting exercise is the **asymmetric cost** of being wrong. Under-predicting pollution in a residential zone — telling people the air is cleaner than it is — is far more harmful than a cautious over-prediction, so in this problem it is penalised 50× more heavily. A model that just reports its posterior mean is implicitly assuming symmetric costs, which here would be a quietly dangerous default. Instead I let the model spend its own uncertainty: in residential areas it shifts predictions upward by **1.1 × the posterior standard deviation**, deliberately erring high exactly where a mistake is expensive and staying neutral where it is not. The GP's uncertainty estimate stops being decoration and becomes the mechanism that buys safety.

The model uses a ConstantKernel × anisotropic RBF kernel plus a WhiteKernel noise term in **scikit-learn**. A single exact GP does not scale — its cost grows with the cube of the number of points, which a city's worth of measurements quickly overwhelms. So I cover the map with a **grid of local GPs**, each responsible for its own patch, and use a **Nyström low-rank feature map** to keep the linear algebra tractable on tens of thousands of readings.

The local-GP choice was driven by the data, not by taste: the measurements are strongly bimodal — quiet background areas against sharp pollution corridors tracing the road network — and one global length scale cannot be right for both. Fitting locally lets each region find its own smoothness. This was the project where cost-sensitive prediction and probabilistic surrogates over sparse sensor data first clicked for me, and I have reached for that combination repeatedly since.
