A **Gaussian Process** regression model for predicting **PM2.5** concentrations in residential areas, with calibrated uncertainty estimates.

The interesting part is the **asymmetric cost**: under-predicting pollution in a residential zone is penalized 50× more heavily than over-predicting it. Rather than reporting the posterior mean, the model shifts its predictions in residential areas upward by 1.1 × the posterior standard deviation, spending the GP's own uncertainty estimate to buy safety where mistakes are expensive.

The model uses a ConstantKernel × anisotropic RBF kernel plus a WhiteKernel noise term in **scikit-learn**. To scale beyond what a single exact GP can handle, I cover the full city with a **grid of local GPs** combined with a **Nyström low-rank feature map**, keeping training tractable on tens of thousands of measurements.
